How far is a village?

To learn whether rainfall or market access explains child malnutrition in a place, you first have to decide how much land around that place counts. Survey coordinates are deliberately moved before release, so the honest answer is: it depends on two different things at once.

Why one number cannot do both jobs

Household survey locations are shifted by up to 2 km in towns and 5 km in the countryside, with a small share moved as far as 10 km. Nobody outside the survey agency knows the direction or the distance.

So the circle you draw has to be wide enough to contain the village wherever it really sits. That argues for a big radius. But it also has to describe the land people actually use: fields within walking distance, the nearest market, the catchment a river drains. That argues for a small one, and the right size differs for rainfall, for roads, and for each of the three conditions I am mapping.

Widening the circle until the village is certainly inside it means most of what you measure is somewhere else. That is the trade the sliders show, and it is why my thesis tests several radii for every variable instead of picking one.
